Humans

Fragile by comparison, relentless by necessity, and foundational to the Federation’s survival.

History

Among all peoples of Alterra, Humans were once considered the most fragile. Their lives were short, their bodies lacked the specialized adaptations of other races, and their kingdoms often rose and fell within a few generations. What Humans lacked in permanence they compensated for with relentless adaptability.

Human civilization spread across the central plains before the Endless War, building trade cities and agricultural centers between older powers. The old Human Kingdoms were ruled by regional Eparchs who swore fealty to the Solar Throne of Valedorn, though internal maneuvering made Humanity nearly as fractured as the surrounding world.

Humans became masters of logistics and statecraft out of necessity. Their cities were built to be rebuilt, their armies valued flexibility over tradition, and their diplomats learned to navigate conflicting customs simply to survive between larger powers.

When the Cataclysm tore reality open, Human kingdoms suffered catastrophic losses. Entire noble lines vanished, central cities were consumed by instability, and refugees flooded surviving settlements. Eparch Claudius of Valedorn became one of the first leaders to answer Queen A’hmynda’s call for unity, recognizing that no kingdom could survive the new world alone.

Human engineers, diplomats, and laborers became foundational in Union City and early Federation infrastructure. Some historians say the Elves conceived the Federation and the Dwarves built it, but Humanity organized it into something capable of surviving.

Culture

Modern Human culture is defined less by shared ancestry than by shared ambition. No race has integrated more thoroughly into multicultural Federation society. Human communities can be found almost everywhere in Alterra, adapting quickly to local customs while influencing them in return.

Because of this, Humans are often seen as cultural mirrors. A Human raised in a Dwarven district may value craft and stability, one raised near Orc communities may embrace communal discipline, and one born in Union City may think of themselves as Federation first.

This flexibility makes Humans especially suited to administration, commerce, diplomacy, and entrepreneurship. At the same time, traditionalists criticize Humans for abandoning historical customs too easily. Human cities often blend Dwarven crystal broadcasts, Orcish music, Elven-inspired gardens, Halfling airship docks, and temples to foreign deities into ordinary life.

Humans exist in contradiction. They are among the strongest proponents of unity, but their surviving noble houses are also capable of politically weaponizing division.

Naming Conventions

Human naming varies by region and cultural influence. Most Humans possess a given name followed by a familial surname inherited from one or both parents. Older noble families retain elaborate surnames tied to territories or deeds, while common Federation citizens often use simpler blended names. Feminine names include Elena, Cassandra, Lyra, and Vivienne. Masculine names include Adrian, Lucien, Marcus, and Elias. Family names include Valebrook, Thorne, Ashford, and Castellan.

Physicality

Humans possess the widest physical variation of any race in Alterra. Their adaptability and multicultural integration have produced enormous diversity in appearance, body type, and complexion. Human builds range from slender and wiry to broad and muscular depending on region and lifestyle.

Unlike more specialized peoples, Humans are defined by versatility rather than a singular adaptation. What others once viewed as weakness has become their evolutionary strength, allowing them to survive almost anywhere and adjust quickly to changing technology and culture.

Archetypes

Diplomats thrive in the tangled social and political systems of the Federation. Diplomacy, Persuasion, Observation, and Culture allow them to wield words as deftly as others wield blades.

Pioneers embody Human adaptability. Inventors, settlers, freelancers, and entrepreneurs use Survival, Appraisal, Artifice, and Discipline to carve opportunity from uncertainty.

Luminaries shape the cultural identity of the modern Federation through art, journalism, broadcasting, and public speech. Performance, Empathy, Lore, and Charm are their foundation.

Soldiers place duty above personal ambition. As peacekeepers, officers, and community defenders, they rely on Leadership, Defense, Discipline, and Vigor.
