Elves

Long-lived magical architects of the old world, burdened by the Cataclysm and divided over redemption or restoration.

History

Before the Federation, the Elven Kingdoms stood as the dominant magical power of Alterra. Ancient Elven civilization treated magic as the highest expression of order and enlightenment, believing mastery of mana proved cultural and intellectual superiority.

The earliest Elven cities were built deep in forest canopies and around leyline nexuses where ambient mana flowed strongly. From these centers, Elves cultivated arcane sciences far beyond other races. Their pursuit of magical perfection eventually hardened into arrogance.

Elven leaders increasingly believed themselves rightful shepherds of civilization and sought control over resources needed to maintain magical dominance. This led to war with the Dwarven Syndicate over mineral-rich mountain territories. Though Elves held overwhelming magical power, Dwarven weaponry prevented total conquest. The eventual exchange of magic for resources sparked a golden age of advancement but accelerated the arms race that became the Endless War.

As tensions escalated, Elven research became militarized. King T’ilear IV ordered creation of a spell meant to annihilate kingdoms by siphoning and detonating ambient mana. Instead, the spell tore open reality, creating the Rift and unleashing horrors beyond mortal comprehension.

Modern Elves carry this guilt heavily. Queen A’hmynda, T’ilear’s successor, helped found the Federation to prevent history from repeating itself. Since then, Elven culture has split between those seeking redemption through unity and those who believe the Elves surrendered their rightful place atop civilization.

Culture

Modern Elves possess one of the strongest tensions between tradition and progress in the Federation. To outsiders they often appear graceful, sophisticated, and emotionally composed. In truth, Elven culture places immense pressure on self-control and excellence. Emotional restraint is seen as maturity, while impulsiveness is associated with irresponsibility and historical shame.

Because Elves live far longer than Humans, their perspective on time differs dramatically. Projects may span decades, relationships develop slowly, and grudges last generations. This longevity makes them exceptional scholars, mages, artists, and strategists, but it also makes rapid social change difficult.

Younger Elves increasingly struggle against rigidity. Magitech, multicultural integration, and Federation identity have created unrest among those frustrated with old noble houses. Some embrace innovation, while others feel trapped between reverence for tradition and fear of repeating ancestral mistakes.

Elves possess a deep reverence for beauty and symbolism. Clothing, architecture, speech, and even silence carry layers of meaning. To an Elf, aesthetics are not decoration. They are reflections of discipline and intentionality.

Naming Conventions

Elven names are melodic and flowing, often inspired by stars, seasons, emotions, and natural phenomena. Noble houses maintain ancestral surnames tied to ancient lineages and territories. Feminine names include Sylvaine, Elyria, Vaelora, and Seris. Masculine names include Caelum, Aelion, Thandor, and Luceris. Family names include Dawnmere, Valtheris, Moonveil, and Basch.

Physicality

Elves are typically tall and slender with sharp facial features and naturally graceful movement. Their long lifespans and connection to mana often give them an ethereal appearance.

Many possess elongated ears, luminous eyes, exceptionally smooth skin, and uncommon hair colors. Their bodies are naturally attuned to mana flow, making them gifted in arcane disciplines. Even non-mages often display heightened reflexes and awareness.

Archetypes

Spellblades blend martial training with arcane enhancement, treating combat as both science and art through Blades, Arcana, Athletics, and Discipline.

Arcanists focus on magical mastery and academic excellence through Lore, Research, Spellcraft, and Ritualism.

Keepers preserve Elven history, tradition, and cultural memory. Culture, Lore, Empathy, and Observation guide their work.

Vanguards are younger Elves who reject stagnation and push society forward through Leadership, Artifice, Persuasion, and Discipline.
