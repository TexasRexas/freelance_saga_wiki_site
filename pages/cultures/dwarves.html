Dwarves

Mountain-born inventors, crystal crafters, clan traditionalists, and caretakers of songs nearly lost to the Cataclysm.

History

The Dwarves of the past operated through a triumvirate consul made of the heads of the three great clans: Goldheart, Silverhall, and Copperhearth. The Goldhearts were traditionally builders and planners, the Silverhalls were providers of food and raw resources, and the Copperhearths were caretakers of culture and negotiation. Together these clans formed the Dwarven Syndicate, with smaller clans growing out of the needs and specializations of the three great houses.

Ancient Dwarves settled the mountains of Eastern Alterra and carved elaborate cliffside cities into the highest peaks. Their capital of Vulheim was said to be the most beautiful of all, a city whose tunnels and open faces sang when wind moved through the mountain. Their reputation for invention, craft, and engineering spread so widely that other races often sought Dwarven weapons and armor for their war campaigns.

When the Elves declared war to claim mountain resources, the Syndicate’s lack of a single central home became its greatest weakness. Superior weaponry allowed the Dwarves to stall the Elven advance until a tense agreement was reached: Dwarves would open trade of raw materials in exchange for magical knowledge. This exchange began a golden age of Dwarven enchanting and transformed their craft into a global industry before the Cataclysm.

During the Endless War, the warm and music-filled mountain halls became fortresses. When Giants breached the mountaintop homes, many Dwarves tunneled downward and built labyrinth cities beneath the earth. There they discovered crystal veins that allowed them to channel magic in new ways. The Cataclysm changed their plans by awakening ravenous things below the surface, forcing them to abandon their homes again.

Culture

The Dwarves of the Federation have kept much of their ancestral curiosity and inventiveness. When Union City was first formed, Dwarven creators developed broadcasting magic that could recreate the lost songs of the mountain cities. That technology eventually became the communication and entertainment crystals used across the Federation.

Though the Great Clans hold little formal authority, their names still carry enormous cultural weight. Dwarven interests are well represented in the Council, and many Dwarves experience a level of privilege unavailable to less established peoples. This security has allowed Dwarven families and corporations to dominate luxury industries, especially fine goods, entertainment crystals, and long-range communications.

Many Dwarves remain traditionalists, preferring methods that have already proven reliable over new and complex technologies. This is especially common among the old guard and noble-blooded clans. Dwarves further removed from clan prestige are often more comfortable adapting old skills into magitech engineering.

Naming Conventions

Dwarven names consist of a given name of traditional Dwarven origin and a clan name that often references minerals, gems, metals, or stone. Feminine names include Alys, Nia, Bronwen, and Seren. Masculine names include Rhys, Owain, Dylan, and Ieuan. Clan names include Diamondback, Ironside, Steelhammer, and Rubybeard.

Physicality

Hardy and stout are the words most often used to describe Dwarven builds. Adult males average between 5'5 and 5'8, while adult females often fall between 4'10 and 5'3. Dwarves commonly range from stocky to densely muscular, traits shaped by generations of stonework, mining, and life at high altitude.

Life in frigid mountain homes altered Dwarven bodies to retain heat. Thick body hair is common among males, while increased fat cells are more common among females, though some predisposition to both traits is found across Dwarfkind. Their skin tones and hair colors are nearly as varied as those of Humans.

Archetypes

Virtuosos are Dwarves for whom art and culture are a way of life. Whether through art, music, dance, poetry, or storytelling, they preserve and reshape the living culture of their people through Performance, Charm, Deception, and Empathy.

Hearthkeepers are deeply involved in the places they call home. They tend communities through Medicine, Crafts, Culture, and domestic professional talents, keeping daily life functioning even during instability.

Stalwarts rely on dense builds and steady resolve to hold their ground in the face of danger. Defense, Shields, Willpower, and Vigor allow them to weather storms that would break others.
