Ormyri

Dragon-descended people of Highwind, shaped by elemental ancestry, brood legacy, and survival through alliance.

History

Before Humans were born, before Dwarves carved mountains, and before Elves laid their first cities, Dragons ruled Alterra. Born from primordial elemental forces, they shaped terrain to their liking and claimed territories around elemental wells that sustained their immense life force. Their relationship with those wells was nearly parasitic, as they drained raw elemental energy to preserve their immortality.

The age of Dragons eventually ended. Some say the wells weakened under centuries of feeding, while others claim Dragon wars scarred the world. From the remnants of their lineage came the Ormyri, mortal inheritors of fragments of draconic power.

Early Ormyri societies formed around ancient roosts and dormant elemental wells, building towering cities on cliffs, mountains, and floating landmasses where mana currents remained strong. Their greatest city was Highwind. During the Endless War they often sold military expertise while avoiding large-scale involvement, but Orc threats from the north and Lycan pressure from the east made neutrality difficult.

The Cataclysm devastated the already small Ormyri population. Bloodlines vanished defending elemental wells from Rift creatures, and planar instability may have caused infertility and magical sickness. A century after the Federation formed, Queen Ember Crimson petitioned for entrance. The Council accepted unanimously, stabilizing Highwind and bringing Ormyri forces into the defense against the Rift.

Union City built the Upper City to accommodate Ormyri movement and physiology. With protection and resources, their population experienced its first major growth in centuries. For many Ormyri, the Federation did not merely save their civilization. It gave them a future.

Culture

Modern Ormyri culture is caught between ancestral pride and adaptation. Dragonkind is not myth to them; it is ancestry. Every Ormyri is raised to understand that fragments of primordial power live within them, and how they cultivate that power reflects upon the entire brood.

Older Ormyri value discipline, restraint, martial excellence, and reverence toward draconic ancestry. Younger Ormyri, especially those in Union City, increasingly embrace self-expression, performance arts, magitech aviation, and identities outside rigid brood expectations. Ancient sky temples now stand beside neon entertainment towers and flight docks.

Elemental affinity shapes social expectation. Fire-aligned Ormyri are often expected to be passionate leaders, while wind-aligned Ormyri may be encouraged toward curiosity and freedom. Many younger Ormyri resist these assumptions, but the cultural weight remains strong.

The Dragonguard remain deeply respected, originally defending ancient roosts and now protecting Highwind and Federation airspace. Broods function as extended family units, so achievements and failures alike reflect upon the group. This can create immense pressure for anyone who feels disconnected from expectation.

Naming Conventions

Ormyri names consist of a given name associated with the elemental force their lineage resonates with and a brood name tied to the exact shade of their scales. Fire names include Ember, Cinder, Solis, and Pyra. Wind names include Zephyr, Gale, Tempest, and Stratos. Water names include Tide, Cascade, Nerissa, and Mist. Earth names include Flint, Obsidian, Terra, and Quake. Brood names include Crimson, Azure, Onyx, Ivory, Viridian, Saffron, Umber, and Cerulean.

Physicality

Ormyri are humanoid draconic beings with scaled patches, slit-pupil eyes, pronounced canines, clawed hands, and horns or crest-like protrusions. Many possess tails that vary by brood. Their scale coloration is hereditary and treated as a marker of lineage and social identity.

Elemental magic subtly affects Ormyri bodies. Fire-aligned Ormyri often run warm, wind-aligned Ormyri tend toward lighter frames and heightened reflexes, earth-aligned Ormyri commonly develop denser scales and musculature, and water-aligned Ormyri often possess smoother scales and greater breath control.

Archetypes

Dragonguard dedicate themselves to defending their people and Federation skies through Defense, Polearms, Discipline, and Leadership.

Skyriders embrace freedom, exploration, and aerial mastery as couriers, racers, explorers, and pilots using Vehicles, Athletics, Observation, and Survival.

Wyrmsingers channel elemental resonance through music, dance, poetry, or magical performance. Performance, Arcana, Empathy, and Culture allow them to captivate audiences.

Emberwrights blend draconic elemental knowledge with magitech engineering through Artifice, Crafting, Lore, and Research.
